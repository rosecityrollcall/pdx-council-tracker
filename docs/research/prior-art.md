*Research date: August 8, 2026. All GitHub dates verified via the GitHub API. Direct fetches that failed bot-blocking are flagged.*

## 1. Councilmatic family / Open Civic Data â€” status in 2026

**Verdict: the ecosystem is alive but Chicago-centric and heavier than a volunteer Portland site needs. The OCD *schema* is the reusable part; the Django stack mostly isn't.**

- **django-councilmatic** ([github.com/datamade/django-councilmatic](https://github.com/datamade/django-councilmatic)) â€” Django app providing models/views for bills, people, organizations, events; "lightly extends the Open Civic Data Standard, implemented in Django as python-opencivicdata." Known instances: Philly (original), Chicago, LA Metro. Not archived; default branch is `3.x`, last push **Aug 8, 2025**, 42 open issues ([api.github.com/repos/datamade/django-councilmatic](https://api.github.com/repos/datamade/django-councilmatic)). - **chi-councilmatic** ([github.com/datamade/chi-councilmatic](https://github.com/datamade/chi-councilmatic)) â€” the reference living deployment: Django + OCD, Legistar-API scrapers, PostgreSQL, **nightly GitHub Actions updates**, plus a public **Datasette SQLite mirror** at [puddle.datamade.us/chicago_council](https://puddle.datamade.us/chicago_council) ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/datamade/chi-councilmatic/main/README.md)). - **pupa** (scraping framework) â€” last push **July 31, 2026**, not archived ([api.github.com/repos/opencivicdata/pupa](https://api.github.com/repos/opencivicdata/pupa)). **python-opencivicdata** â€” last push **Nov 11, 2024**, not archived ([api.github.com/repos/opencivicdata/python-opencivicdata](https://api.github.com/repos/opencivicdata/python-opencivicdata)) â€” maintenance-mode.
- **OCD VoteEvent schema** ([open-civic-data.readthedocs.io/en/latest/data/vote.html](https://open-civic-data.readthedocs.io/en/latest/data/vote.html)) â€” defines: `organization`, `motion`, `date`, `type` (bill-passage / amendment-passage / etc.), `passed` (bool), `vote_counts` (`{vote_type: yes|no|absent|abstain|not voting|other, count}`), `roll_call` (per-person `{person, vote_type}`), optional `bill` link, required `sources` URLs. - **NYC Councilmatic** was adopted as the official NYC Council site (**Dec 2017**, [laws.council.nyc.gov](https://laws.council.nyc.gov/)) and the nonprofit instance shut down ([Medium: David Moore](https://ppolitics.medium.com/councilmatic-is-now-an-official-nyc-council-website-e6ddc542fe73)); `nyc.councilmatic.org` no longer resolves in DNS (confirmed ENOTFOUND on fetch).

## 2. Feature survey â€” what best communicates voting to laypeople

- **They Vote For You** (OpenAustralia Foundation, [theyvoteforyou.org.au](https://theyvoteforyou.org.au/) â€” direct fetch 403'd, bot-blocked; features per [openaustraliafoundation.org.au](https://openaustraliafoundation.org.au/projects/they-vote-for-you), [FAQ](https://theyvoteforyou.org.au/help/faq), [source](https://github.com/openaustralia/theyvoteforyou)): the gold standard for lay readability. Its killer moves: (a) **curated "policies"** â€” plain-English issue stances ("voted consistently for building more public housing") computed from grouped divisions, rather than raw bill lists; (b) **a plain-English explanation of every division**; (c) **compare any two politicians by vote similarity**; (d) flagging when a member breaks with their party/bloc.
- **GovTrack** ([govtrack.us/about/analysis](https://www.govtrack.us/about/analysis)): **annual report cards** per legislator ([example: 2024 House ideology](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/members/report-cards/2024/house/ideology)) â€” ideology score from cosponsorship patterns, leadership score, **missed-votes/attendance relative to peers**. Lesson: small per-member stat cards ("missed 4% of votes, more than 80% of colleagues") communicate instantly; abstract ideology scatterplots less so.
- **Chicago Councilmatic** ([chicago.councilmatic.org](https://chicago.councilmatic.org/)): legislation search, committee & member pages, and the Councilmatic signature â€” **free email alerts on bills/members/search terms** ([per the NYC adoption writeup](https://ppolitics.medium.com/councilmatic-is-now-an-official-nyc-council-website-e6ddc542fe73)), plus the Datasette raw-data mirror for journalists.
- **Rose City Reform's static charts** (see Â§3): its **pairwise agreement chart** of the 12 Portland councilors was the most-shared artifact of its coverage ([The New Kids on the Bloc](https://rosecityreform.substack.com/p/the-new-kids-on-the-bloc)) â€” direct evidence that an **agreement matrix** resonates for this specific council.

## 3. Does anything already track the new Portland council? (the gap)

- **Official â€” portland.gov/council/votes** ([portland.gov/council/votes](https://www.portland.gov/council/votes)): roll-call records **Jan 6, 2021 â†’ present**, 15,569 vote records across 649 pages; per-record fields: document number (e.g. 2026-189), title, member, vote (Yea/Nay/Absent/Abstain); searchable by title/doc number/member. **Server-rendered (WebFetch got full content â€” no JS wall), but no API, CSV, or bulk export.** The city's open-data listing offers nothing more for council votes ([portland.gov/public-records/open-data](https://www.portland.gov/public-records/open-data)).
- **Official â€” new "Council Documents" tool, launched July 2, 2026** ([announcement](https://www.portland.gov/council/news/2026/7/2/new-online-tool-offers-earlier-visibility-council-legislation-and-schedules), tool at [portland.gov/council/documents](https://www.portland.gov/council/documents)): filter legislation by councilor, committee, department, scheduled date; shows items **before formal scheduling** and tentative schedules **12 days ahead, updated Fridays 9 AM**. This is a pipeline for "upcoming votes," not a tracker of outcomes.
- **Rose City Reform** (Maja Viklands Harris; [rosecityreform.org](https://rosecityreform.org/) â€” direct fetch 403'd/bot-blocked; Substack readable): the closest existing thing, but it's **periodic research journalism, not a browsable tracker**. Its 2025 wrap-up ([The New Normal](https://rosecityreform.substack.com/p/the-new-normal)) analyzed 187 non-unanimous votes: 42.1% of all 2025 votes non-unanimous (617% over 2024), ~80% of *final* votes still unanimous, budget = 46% of disagreements, Progressive Caucus ("Peacock", 6 members: Avalos, Dunphy, Green, Kanal, Koyama Lane, Morillo) membership the top predictor of voting behavior, bloc members in the majority 91% of non-unanimous votes vs 58% for others ([The New Kids on the Bloc](https://rosecityreform.substack.com/p/the-new-kids-on-the-bloc), [A Year of Good Intentions](https://rosecityreform.substack.com/p/a-year-of-good-intentions), [Building Blocs report page](https://rosecityreform.org/building-blocs/)). Charts are **static graphics in articles**; no per-member lookup, no continuous updates.
- **BikePortland**: active narrative coverage of council/committee action (e.g. [transportation committee formation](https://bikeportland.org/2025/01/16/transportation-committee-now-set-despite-concerns-from-district-2-councilor-392281), [12-hour budget meeting with 120+ amendments](https://bikeportland.org/2025/01/27/city-council-reckons-with-infrastructure-392386)) â€” journalism, no structured vote data.
- **GitHub**: exactly one relevant repo â€” **pfarnach/pdx-city-council-vote-scraper** ([github.com/pfarnach/pdx-city-council-vote-scraper](https://github.com/pfarnach/pdx-city-council-vote-scraper)): TypeScript/Cheerio scraper of portland.gov/council/votes producing per-councilor JSON tallies + terminal charts. Created and last pushed **May 1, 2026** (same day), 2 commits, 0 stars, no persistence, no site ([API](https://api.github.com/repos/pfarnach/pdx-city-council-vote-scraper)). Value: **proof the official votes page is cleanly scrapable**, not a competitor. Note: a search result claimed a `portland-dsa/city-council-vote-tracker` repo; **it does not exist** (404; the [Portland DSA org](https://github.com/orgs/portland-dsa/repositories) has 3 unrelated repos).

**The gap, precisely:** the city publishes raw roll-calls with no export and no aggregation; Rose City Reform aggregates brilliantly but only in point-in-time PDFs/posts; nobody offers a continuously updated, per-member, per-vote, layperson-readable site. The field is open, and with **6 of 12 seats up Nov 3, 2026** ([Wikipedia: 2026 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Portland,_Oregon_City_Council_election)), demand is peaking.

## 4. Recommendations

**Data model â€” adopt OCD's *vocabulary*, not its stack.** The [VoteEvent schema](https://open-civic-data.readthedocs.io/en/latest/data/vote.html) (motion, result, `vote_counts`, per-person `roll_call`, `sources`, bill link) maps 1:1 onto what portland.gov/council/votes exposes, and using its field names keeps you interoperable with Councilmatic tooling and legible to civic-tech contributors. But skip django-councilmatic/python-opencivicdata/pupa for a 12-member council â€” that stack assumes Legistar and a Postgres+Django ops burden (chi-councilmatic needs Docker, Heroku, nightly Actions: [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/datamade/chi-councilmatic/main/README.md)). Instead: **nightly scraper (extend pfarnach's Cheerio approach) â†’ SQLite/JSON in the repo â†’ static site build**, with OCD-shaped records: `people` (with district + term start/end dates â€” memberships change after Nov 2026), `vote_events` (doc number, title, date, result, counts), `votes` (person, cast: yea/nay/absent/abstain). Publish the SQLite via Datasette like DataMade's [puddle](https://puddle.datamade.us/chicago_council) for journalists.

**Five highest-value features for a small volunteer site** (ranked):
1. **12Ã—12 agreement matrix, continuously updated, non-unanimous votes only** â€” proven resonance via Rose City Reform's static version ([source](https://rosecityreform.substack.com/p/the-new-kids-on-the-bloc)); filtering to non-unanimous is essential since ~80% of final votes are unanimous ([source](https://rosecityreform.substack.com/p/the-new-normal)).
2. **Per-member pages with plain-English vote lines** ("Voted NO on the $8.6B budget, which passed 7â€“5") plus GovTrack-style stat cards: absences vs peers, % of divided votes in the majority, agreement-with-bloc rate ([GovTrack model](https://www.govtrack.us/about/analysis)).
3. **Divided-votes feed** â€” reverse-chron list of only contested votes with margin badges (7â€“5, 6â€“6) and a one-sentence lay summary of the motion; this is the "what actually split the council" view no one offers.
4. **Issue tags â†’ They-Vote-For-You-style "policies"** â€” hand-tag divided votes (housing, police, budget, transportation) so each member gets "voted consistently for/against X" summaries; curation is the volunteer-labor feature that machines can't fake ([TVFY model](https://openaustraliafoundation.org.au/projects/they-vote-for-you)).
5. **Upcoming-votes alerts** â€” weekly email/RSS built from the city's new [Council Documents](https://www.portland.gov/council/documents) 12-day lookahead (updated Fridays 9 AM, [announcement](https://www.portland.gov/council/news/2026/7/2/new-online-tool-offers-earlier-visibility-council-legislation-and-schedules)), closing the loop from "what's coming" to "how they voted" â€” the Councilmatic feature users historically loved most ([alerts precedent](https://ppolitics.medium.com/councilmatic-is-now-an-official-nyc-council-website-e6ddc542fe73)).

**JS-rendering caveats:** none blocking â€” portland.gov votes/documents pages are server-rendered.
